# California Coastal Road Trip Planning

## Choosing Your Route
The classic coastal run follows Highway 1 between San Francisco and Los
Angeles, but few people drive the whole thing in one go. Most travelers
split it into two- or three-day legs, anchoring around Big Sur, San
Luis Obispo, and Santa Barbara. Driving south to north is generally
preferred if you want the ocean-side lane, since Highway 1 hugs the
coast on the right when heading north.

## Best Time of Year to Go
Late spring (May-June) and early fall (September-October) offer the
clearest skies and fewest crowds. Summer brings marine layer fog that
can blanket the coast, especially in the mornings, and burns off by
early afternoon most days. Winter storms occasionally close sections of
Highway 1 near Big Sur due to landslides, so it's worth checking road
conditions before a winter trip.

## Where to Stop Along the Way
Big Sur is the highlight for most travelers, with McWay Falls and
Bixby Creek Bridge as the two most-photographed stops. Further south,
Hearst Castle near San Simeon is worth a half-day detour inland.
Santa Barbara makes a good overnight base, with a walkable downtown
and easy beach access. Morro Bay, with its distinctive volcanic rock
just offshore, is an underrated stop most people drive past.

## Budgeting for the Trip
Lodging is the biggest variable cost, ranging from budget motels
around $150/night to oceanfront hotels well over $500/night in Big
Sur specifically, where options are limited and demand is high.
Gas and a rental car typically run $60-100/day combined. Building in
one splurge night (usually Big Sur) and economizing on the rest keeps
the overall trip budget reasonable without sacrificing the best views.

## Packing Essentials
Layers matter more than most people expect, since coastal temperatures
can swing 20 degrees between morning fog and afternoon sun. A phone
mount and offline maps are worth having, since cell service drops out
for long stretches through Big Sur. Motion sickness medication is
worth packing too — the road's constant curves catch some passengers
off guard even on short legs.
